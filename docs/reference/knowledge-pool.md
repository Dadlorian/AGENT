# Knowledge pool — what stands behind every card

Generated by `python3 tools/knowledge_pool.py --write`. Every figure is a join against a real record, not a score. **Re-run it rather than trusting these numbers later.**

This was two documents — one asking whether a card was *covered*, one asking whether its evidence was *trustworthy*. They drifted, their percentages looked alike, and the difference got read wrong. It is one document now for that reason.

## 1. Every quote, typed — the number to act on

Not whether a citation passed, but **what kind of difference** there is between the sentence a card asserts and the page it names. A bare true/false scored a flattened bullet list and an invented statistic identically.

| Verdict | Meaning | Quotes | Share | Gate |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `exact` | the quote is byte-identical on the page | 217 | 92.7% | passes |
| `formatting` | same words; markdown, a flattened list, line numbers or a pronoun differ | 7 | 3.0% | passes |
| `unretrievable` | the page could not be read - proves nothing either way | 6 | 2.6% | recorded |
| `partial` | part is on the page, part is not - **a person must read this one** | 3 | 1.3% | blocks until reviewed |
| `stitched` | two non-adjacent passages joined into one sentence | 1 | 0.4% | blocks |

**4 of 234 quotes need a human decision.** The system settled the rest on its own. No check can separate a faithful paraphrase of a page from an invented quote — both share the source's vocabulary — so `partial` is deliberately a *needs-a-reader* bucket, not a verdict.

23 citations predating this rule are enumerated in `docs/reference/citation-debt.json`. That file is debt, not permission: nothing writes to it automatically.
