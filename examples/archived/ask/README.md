# ask — I have intent; how do I hand it over, and what do I get back at once?

Four producers — a person at a shell or an IDE, an event, a timer, a partner
agent — express one job four different ways. One boundary normalises all four
into one envelope, derives the key and the correlation the producer never sent,
resolves the acting identity and the chain behind it, takes one admission
decision before anything is spent, claims the key, records one entry, and hands
back one acknowledgement. Nothing runs. `bash examples/ask/test.sh` is the
visible check.

Every row below is cited to a knowledge-base id, a research id, or a file with a
verbatim quote, or it says **proposed** and gives its reasoning. Product names
appear only in the standards table and the adapters table.

## 3. The call

Four entries cover nearly every situation and all four enter through the same
shape (`T-t6-02`). What a producer sends is in its own native format
(`producers.json`); what the boundary produces is the entry document
(`entries/<door>.json`), and `test.sh` step 1 asserts the boundary reproduces
each of those four documents exactly from the native message. No client library
this repository wrote is involved at any door (`F-b1-05` "A caller needs no
client library we wrote.").

| Door | The one line a caller writes | What the producer actually sends | The document behind it | What is different | What is identical |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| human — a shell or an IDE | `python3 examples/ask/run.py --entry examples/ask/entries/human.json` | `cli-argv`: an argv with `--workflow`, `--summary`, `--report`, `--window-hours`, plus its own `request_id` | `entries/human.json` — `user:corey`, one hop, attested by the door it came in at | the key is derived from the request identifier; the producer holds the submission open | the envelope shape, the job digest, the resolved manifest, every stamped concern |
| event | `python3 examples/ask/run.py --entry examples/ask/entries/event.json` | `cloudevents-http`: `specversion`, `type`, `source`, `id`, `time`, `subject`, `data` | `entries/event.json` — `service:alerting`, two hops, rooted in the principal that authorised the subscription | the key is derived from the event envelope's own `source` and `id`; the body's `claimed_submitter` is dropped, never believed | the envelope shape, the job digest, the resolved manifest, every stamped concern |
| schedule | `python3 examples/ask/run.py --entry examples/ask/entries/schedule.json` | `schedule-occurrence`: the recurring item's identity, its `recurrence`, the nominal `occurrence`, the `received_at` wall clock | `entries/schedule.json` — `schedule:nightly-intake-sweep`, two hops, rooted in the human who owns the schedule | the key is derived from (schedule identity, nominal occurrence) and **not** from `received_at`, so a catch-up delivery of one occurrence is one entry (`test.sh` step 3) | the envelope shape, the job digest, the resolved manifest, every stamped concern |
| external — another agent | `python3 examples/ask/run.py --entry examples/ask/entries/external.json` | `a2a-message`: `messageId`, `taskId`, `contextId`, typed parts, one of them the job | `entries/external.json` — `agent:partner-sre-bot`, three hops, `parent_correlation_id`, `depth: 1` | the submitting agent's own `contextId` becomes the **parent** correlation, never this run's; the body's `submittedBy` is dropped | the envelope shape, the job digest, the resolved manifest, every stamped concern |
| all four at once | `python3 examples/ask/run.py --all-doors` | — | the four documents above | — | one job digest, one resolved manifest, four entry ids, four correlation ids, four executing identities, four keys (`test.sh` step 1) |
| what an outside caller reads first | `python3 examples/ask/run.py --describe` | — | the capability description: the schema and dialect, the four doors, what the boundary sets, the lifecycle states, the retention window, the failure modes | — | — |

The declaration behind all of it is `admission.json`: the schema and dialect,
the registered producers and what authorised each one, the members of a
producer's message identity the key is derived from, the delegation bound, the
scope ladder, the decision point, the retention window, the lifecycle states and
the ceiling. Adding a producer is a row in that file plus one mapper — never a
new door (`FILE:docs/litmus/questionnaire.json#work-intake.future_state`
"Adding a producer is adding a producer, never a new door.").

### The failure modes this example produces

Only these six, and `test.sh` step 12 asserts the set the capability description
advertises is exactly the set the run actually produced — neither a longer list
nor a shorter one.

| `type` | Status | Raised when | Ends the unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `urn:agentic:problem:policy-denied` | 403 | an unregistered producer format submits; a delegation chain is longer than the declared bound; the admission decision denies (here: a resource in another tenant) | yes, before any entry or claim |
| `urn:agentic:problem:document-invalid` | 422 | the envelope the mapper produced fails the published schema; the body carries one located violation per failure, an instance location and a keyword location | yes, before any entry or claim |
| `urn:agentic:problem:dialect-unsupported` | 422 | the declared schema does not declare the 2020-12 dialect; refused before any instance is checked | yes, before any entry or claim |
| `urn:agentic:problem:budget-exhausted` | 402 | the shortest finishing path prices above the ceiling the platform stamped | yes, before any entry or claim |
| `urn:agentic:problem:idempotency-conflict` | 409 | the same derived key arrives with a different job | yes; the first entry stands and the second is never admitted |
| `urn:agentic:problem:adapter-unavailable` | 503 | the recording path is unreachable; the claim already taken is released so the key is not wedged | yes; retryable, and nothing was recorded |

## 5. Composition

| Piece | What it is here | Built from | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| the boundary | `accept` → one canonical envelope, `admit` → one acknowledgement. Both are the capability interface's concrete template methods: the unmapped-producer refusal, the schema check and the replay check run in front of any binding of ours | `harness/work-intake/interface.py`, called through `harnesses.py` | `F-b1-02` "The core imports interfaces, never implementations." |
| the mapper | one function per registered producer format, producing the two members of the job and nothing else; what it did not read is reported as dropped, computed from what it declares it reads | `doors.py:MAPPERS`, `doors.py:dropped_by` | `X-litmus-b-004` "not in copying the required attribute names" |
| the derivation | the key composed at the boundary from the members of the producer's own message identity the declaration names for that format | `doors.py:derive_key` | `X-litmus-c-012` "The event envelope identifier is the settling dedup key, and retention shorter than the producer retry window silently reprocesses." |
| the chain | built from the identity the transport attested plus the principals the declaration registered behind that producer; nothing the producer's body claims about itself is read | `doors.py:chain_for` | `FILE:docs/litmus/questionnaire.json#concern-identity.future_state` "a caller can neither supply, forge, omit nor overwrite it" |
| sequence | map and derive → validate → resolve identity → decide → price → claim → record → acknowledge, in that order, so every refusal above the claim leaves no entry and no key held | `run.py:Admission.submit` | `F-b4-04` "Refusal is deterministic and happens before execution, not after spend" |
| the pure read | the resolved manifest, a pure function of the envelope evaluated outside every adapter, so the doors are comparable without running anything | `harness/work-intake/interface.py:resolve_manifest` | `F-b1-06` "Planning is a pure function and completes before execution begins." |
| the claim | claim → the effect only if fresh → complete, with the acknowledgement stored as the claim's outcome, so a replay is answered from the record and not from the effect | `run.py:Admission.claim_and_record` | `X-litmus-c-011` "storing the outcome in the same transaction as the side effect" |
| the audit | one hash-chained record per submission, admitted or refused, written by the reference example's ledger rather than a second one; what it does not give is a signature an outside verifier could check, which is gap 4 | `examples/end-to-end/run.py:Ledger`, called not copied | `FILE:examples/end-to-end/run.py` "Every step lands here before the run is allowed to call it done." |

## 6. Extension points

Every row is proved by a differential run inside `test.sh`: the same door twice,
one declared value changed, the two records asserted different in the named way.
A green run at the default value proves the default path and nothing else
(`F-a7-03` "A deterministic gate can be structurally green and mean nothing.").

| Where a builder adds | Without touching | How | The differential that proves it is read |
|---|---|---|---|
| A new producer | `run.py`, the entry documents, any other door | add a row to `producers` in `admission.json` and one mapper to `doors.MAPPERS` | `test.sh` step 9: the four registered formats admit; a fifth format at the same door is refused 403 `refuse-unmapped-producer`, with the registered four named in the body and nothing recorded |
| A different deduplication identity | `run.py`, `doors.py` | change `key_fields` for that producer format | `test.sh` step 3: with `["schedule","occurrence"]` a catch-up delivery is one key and one entry; with `["schedule","occurrence","received_at"]` the same two deliveries are two keys and two entries |
| A different delegation bound | anything | change `identity.max_delegation_depth` | `test.sh` step 6: the external door's three hops are admitted at 4 and refused at 2, `rule_id` `delegation-depth-bound`, no credential issued and nothing recorded |
| A different scope ladder | anything | change `identity.scopes` — one rung per admissible hop, widest first | `test.sh` step 6: with four rungs the external door's three hops hold three strictly narrowing scopes and three shortening lifetimes; with two rungs the same chain is refused 403 `scope-ladder-shorter-than-chain` rather than issued the rung before it |

### Gaps this example exposed

| # | Claim that is not supported here | Research query |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two validator implementations are live in this repository — the reference example's walk and the document-validation capability's — and the boundary runs both on every envelope. That is exactly the closure's own check and this example measures their agreement on five documents, but agreement on five is not conformance: the published language-agnostic suite is not run against either | run the published JSON Schema conformance suite against both validator implementations in this repository and record which of the dialect's mechanisms each one actually serves |
| 2 | The registries disagree. The intake capability's local registry has four rows, the platform's closed registry has ten, and `dialect-unsupported` — which this example produces — has a row in neither of them; `budget-exhausted` and `adapter-unavailable` declare different extension members in the two. A typed refusal a caller cannot look up is only half typed | what governs a closed problem-type registry that several capabilities each keep a copy of: who mints a suffix, how a capability-local registry is reconciled with the platform's, and what a caller does with a type its copy does not carry |
| 3 | Nothing here is durable. The claim store, the intake binding's replay map and the entry record all live in one process, so the replay and conflict results hold within a run and are not evidence about a second process, a restart, or the retention window the declaration states. The lease binding arbitrates a real race between threads, not between machines | measure a keyed claim store under redelivery across processes and a restart: what does the reclaim of a lease held by a dead process cost, and how is the retention window observed rather than declared |
| 4 | Nothing signs anything. The receipt is hash-chained by a ledger this repository wrote, so an outside verifier cannot check an admission with a tool we did not write | what minimal attestation binds an admitted envelope, its derived key and its delegation chain to a submission such that an independently maintained verifier can check it |
| 5 | The acknowledgement is the whole of what comes back. There is no subscription, no streaming path and no cancellation operation in this example, though the agent-messaging standard's distinguishing mechanisms are precisely those; the description advertises the lifecycle and says cancellable, which is a property of the declared states and not of a served operation | what is the minimal subscription and cancellation surface an intake boundary must publish for work that outlives one request, and what does a producer that has detached subscribe with |
| 6 | Registration is per producer format, not per producer. `authorised_by` says which principals stand behind `schedule-occurrence`, not behind one named schedule, so two schedules created by two people are indistinguishable here. | 7 | The delegation chain is built from a registration and from what a dry-run transport says it attested.
